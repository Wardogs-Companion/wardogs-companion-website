## Personal data

The site itself sets no cookies. It uses no advertising, no audience measurement and no tracking, loads nothing from any other site, and has no accounts or forms.

### Settings kept in your browser

To keep your settings and where you are in your visit, the site stores a few values in your browser. They never leave it: the site’s scripts send them nowhere.

- **Until the tab is closed** (sessionStorage): animations, sound and volume, console switched on, intro already seen, sections already opened and the time the console was first opened.
- **For 13 months at most after your last choice** (localStorage): the faction chosen on the radar. After that, the site no longer uses it and deletes it on your next visit.

They only serve what you asked for, so they do not require your consent (French Data Protection Act, article 82). Their names all start with “wardogs”. You can delete or block them in your browser’s settings: the site then still works, without remembering your choices.

### Technical data processed by the host

To send you the pages, Vercel necessarily receives the technical data of each request, including: your IP address (and the approximate location derived from it), the page requested, the date and time, your browser and your system.

- **Why**: to display the site and keep it secure. Legal basis: the legitimate interest in serving a reliable and secure site (GDPR, art. 6(1)(f)).
- **Who receives it**: Vercel, the host. On the free plan used here, Vercel processes this data under its own [privacy policy](https://vercel.com/legal/privacy-policy). In case of suspicious traffic, Vercel may show a verification page; if your browser passes it, it keeps a verification session for one hour.
- **How long**: the publisher can see part of it (IP addresses, pages, browsers, countries) in the Vercel dashboard for 24 hours at most, and keeps no copy. Vercel keeps its own data under its privacy policy.
- **Where**: Vercel is an American company. It states that it is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, recognised by the European Commission’s adequacy decision of 10 July 2023.
